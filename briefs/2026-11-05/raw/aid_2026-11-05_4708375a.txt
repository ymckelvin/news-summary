Amid a deepening bear market for AI/tech (worthy of dark humor ) with Anthropic bonds receiving a “junk” rating and OpenAI fundraise headlines , we continue to see very surprising capability finds in the community, such as today. The best way to put today’s blockbuster paper in context is to recall Karpathy’s autoresearch from a mere 8 months ago , which presaged a summer of recursive self improvement fears : Abstractly, the demonstration is that you can take a sufficiently smart model and through running a series of experiments across a compute cluster, train a model autonomously without human expertise or intervention. However, this is usually done by an involved process of writing PyTorch/Jax code and running compute and watching loss curves and reading evals and optimizing kernels and data and…. Well, last month’s guest Alex Zhang comes back to coauthor a MONSTER discovery: what if you just ask a smart model to directly read and edit the weights of a dumber model? View post on X ↗ In ML this is the equivalent of not looking at source code, just directly seeing the 1’s and 0’s of ( very small ) compiled executables, and being able to 1) tell what they do , 2) oneshot-fix bugs given failed traces or, with 50 steps, improve in a general direction ( possible overstatement here ), 3) writing entire network weights from scratch in one shot , beating some independently trained models. If you believe that LLMs can truly fit and simulate everything given enough parameters (and in fact, LLMs are increasingly better calculators as you grow in size), then something like this was in theory bound to happen, but it is surprising nevertheless to have the ability suddenly emerge, as an independent reproduction seems to already have found: View post on X ↗ Of course the exact thing we are really modeling is some variant of mechanistic interpretability (which could be done by hand ), and right now unlikely to scale to more advanced tasks than simple games and RL policies, but the world has now gone from 0 to 1 on this very new RSI capability…. which will immediately put to use and explored due to its remarkable efficiency. AI Twitter Recap DeepSeek Open-Sources a "Super-agent" Harness Prototype Release : DeepSeek Harness published a long design article ( article ) and the dsh-superagent-prototype repo ( repo link ). An official English version followed ( @tianyi ). Architecture : One long-lived router session talks to humans and hands out work. Isolated workers run tasks in parallel, triggered by GitHub events ( summary ). Claimed results : In DeepSeek's own test, p95 completion time fell from 29.5 to 15.7 min versus a single session using subagents ( summary ). Design rationale from a reviewer : Final reviewer 崔添翼 (@tianyi) says "Super" means a layer above the working agent, not superior intelligence. He frames the core trade-off as cross-task continuity versus throughput ( Zhihu summary ). Stated limits : The harness has no built-in cross-session long-term memory yet. Separating state, compute and environment is mostly unimplemented. The prototype has only been validated in a small, trusted internal environment, and security, privacy and agent-to-agent communication remain open questions. Prior art : He acknowledges that other teams explored similar architectures months earlier. Practitioner reception : @badlogicgames says the design matches where Earendil is heading: a single long-lived entity, ad-hoc recursive delegation and cross-system event triggers. @omarsar0 calls it a "masterclass" in coordination-execution harness design. DeepSeek also says it aims to support pi and other major harnesses with each model generation ( @tianyi ). Critique : @teortaxesTex calls the write-up "something of a hot mess." He argues DeepSeek builds internal crutches around model weaknesses such as hallucination rather than making the system generally robust.
