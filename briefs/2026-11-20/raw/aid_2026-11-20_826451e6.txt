That Is BS Erik rejects the idea that building a company means suffering pain for a big reward at the end. He enjoys work every day, even the hard conversations, and hopes to work on Modal for 20 to 50 years. He has no wish to sell, saying he &#8220;would just go and start Modal 2.&#8221; Why the Days of 90% Software Margins Are Totally Gone Almost no company today has the 90%-plus software margins common in Silicon Valley a decade ago. A dollar paid at the top of the stack flows through applications, models, infrastructure, cloud and NVIDIA, with each layer taking 20% to 30% gross margins. Erik sees Modal reaching 40% to 60% margins long term, but never software margins. Why Inference Alone Will Become Commoditized, and You Have to Have a Suite of Products Around It Selling inference alone is a commodity business with no switching costs and a race to the bottom. Erik argues it must come with post-training, sandboxes, agentic tooling, reinforcement learning, and low-latency and batch inference. Modal has spent five years building that infrastructure, which he calls very hard to copy. Why We Will Have More Not Fewer Engineers in the Future Every time programmers become more efficient, demand for software rises. Erik says the job was never translating English into code but understanding human requirements, and that work keeps moving up the stack, from assembly and punch cards to Python and now agents. He expects software engineers to be fine for the next 10 to 20 years. The Wildest Prediction on the Future of the Inference Market The inference market has grown 10x to 100x bigger than Erik expected, and he predicts it will split into many specialized segments. He sees room for low-latency, high-latency, fine-tuning and post-training offerings, plus new hardware and technologies that need far fewer resources. The $15.75 Billion Company That Has Never Made a Pitch Deck Modal has never made a pitch deck for any of its rounds. Erik gets to know investors well before raising and had spoken with Rich and Gonzalo at Accel for at least a year before the D round, so the deal came together very quickly. Why I Would Invest in Cognition at $80BN At $80B, Cognition looks market correct, and coding agents remain the one thing that never stops working. Cognition got lucky buying the Windsurf team and IP, but its momentum is too strong to sit out. With the fund up 5X on paper, the pitch is to put its last $2B into Cognition. Why I Would Invest in Serval over Cognition and Baseten ServiceNow is an old guard with a sales-led CEO, while Serval has an amazing product and the best enterprise adoption Harry has seen. A 10X looks very likely at Serval, but far harder at Baseten at $30B or Cognition. The catch is Serval only raised $150M, so a fund that must deploy capital still has to do Cognition. Where Is the Risk in the AI Trade Right Now? If AI demand is as strong as people think, every GPU and data center will be needed and CoreWeave&#8217;s bonds will be money good. If demand falls short, the first to suffer will not be Google or Amazon with their big balance sheets, but companies like CoreWeave. Why Large Foundation Model Companies Will Acquire in the Robotic Space Foundation model companies will either build or buy a robotics model. An LLM sells to the 81 million knowledge workers in the US, and a robotics foundation model lets the same company address physical workers too, doubling its TAM in the prospectus. That lure means there could well be acquisitions in the space. Agents Are an Existential Risk to DoorDash DoorDash said AI agents sent only several hundred orders against 3 million deliveries a day, which Jason called a dumb comment. Agents can tell users which restaurants deliver themselves or offer pickup, cutting out platform fees. Rory counters that ordering dinner through an agent is not much easier than using the DoorDash app. Why Pricing Pressure Hit Anthropic in Q3 Anthropic&#8217;s GAAP revenue went from roughly $5B in Q1 to $11B in Q2 and $17B in Q3, so quarter-on-quarter growth fell from about 100% to about 50%, still 5X year-on-year. Jason puts the slowdown down to pricing pressure, with OpenAI selling somewhat equivalent products for 30% off, while demand on OpenRouter has not declined.
