AI Twitter Recap Gemini 4 Carbon Tops Independent Leaderboards, with Access Limited to Cyber Defenders Launch : Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon two weeks after Argon. It is positioned as state of the art on cybersecurity and long-horizon tasks ( @sundarpichai , @GoogleDeepMind ). Claimed cyber results : 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for vulnerability finding, 54.8% on ExploitGym for exploitation and 71.0% on CWE-bench for patching ( @sundarpichai ). Availability : Carbon first goes to trusted cyber defenders through the Fairwind Program, with wider developer access promised "as soon as possible" ( @GoogleAI ). Pricing : Phil Schmid quotes $2/$10 per million input/output tokens with an asterisk ( @_philschmid ). Artificial Analysis lists $4/$20 standard pricing with a 50% launch discount that has no end date. Cached input costs $0.20 per million tokens ( @ArtificialAnlys ). Specs : The model has a 1M-token context window and 1M max output. Inputs are text, image, video and speech; output is text ( @ValsAI ). Safety claims (Google) : Successful cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0 fell to 0.0%, and cheating attempts fell to 0.4%. Unsupported claims are down about 2x versus Argon. Overreach fell 26% and under-service fell 29% ( @OfficialLoganK ). Artificial Analysis : Carbon is #1 on Intelligence Index v5 with a score of 59. It leads 4 of the 9 index evals: HLE-Diamond, TB-Science, AA-Omniscience and AutomationBench ( @ArtificialAnlys ). Coding : On the Coding Agent Index, Carbon scores 59 using Antigravity CLI, versus 62 for Claude Code with Opus 5.5 [max]. It scores 51% on FrontierCode v1.1, versus 56% for Opus. Knowledge work : Carbon reaches 1609 Elo on AA-Briefcase, up 126 points from Argon. It still trails Grok 4.7, Muse Wonder and Kimi K3.1 Pro (1678). Cost : Carbon costs $7.76 per index task, versus $10.04 for Opus 5.5 and $6.25 for GPT-6 Astra. It uses 123k output tokens per task, 29% fewer than Opus. Hallucination : Carbon leads Omniscience with 54. Its hallucination rate rose from Argon's 4% to 11%, which is still the second lowest measured. Opus 5.5 and Astra are both at 50%. Other independent evals : Arena : Carbon is #1 in Text Arena (1528), #2 in Agent Arena at a $1.80 median cost per task, and #4 in Code Arena: WebDev (1768) ( @arena ). Arena attributes the gains over Argon to more praise, more confirmed successes and better steerability ( @arena ). Vals : Carbon is #2 on the Vals Index and #1 on the Finance Index and Legal Research Benchmark. It produces 64% more output tokens than Argon, mostly on finance, tax and legal tasks ( @ValsAI , tokens ). Coding benchmarks : Carbon scores 61.2% on FrontierSWE, behind GPT-6 Astra (65.5), Opus 5.5 (62.3) and Sonnet 5.5 (61.9) ( @ProximalHQ ). It sets a narrow state of the art of 9.6% on SWE-sweep ( @KLieret ). Andon Labs : Carbon is #1 on Vending-Bench 2 ( @andonlabs ). It is also #1 on Blueprint-Bench 2, where it ties the human baseline for 3D spatial understanding ( @andonlabs ). Caveats and reactions : Harness : The coding-agent results were measured in Antigravity CLI, so results in other harnesses are untested ( @Yuchenj_UW ). Hallucination risk : Some users are concerned about hallucination in cyber use ( @scaling01 ). Lab gap : scaling01 thinks Google remains about two months behind the next OpenAI and Anthropic releases ( @scaling01 ). RSI framing : On the earnings call, Sundar Pichai said AI is increasing training and launch velocity. He expects "continued acceleration because of RSI" over the next twelve months ( @AndrewCurran_ ). Unconfirmed : Claims of a stronger unreleased Gemini model are not confirmed ( @kimmonismus ). Internal workflow : A Gemini engineer describes overnight agent loops and swarms that inspect RL data. In that workflow, launches now feel like "periodic checkpoints" ( @zhangqiaorjc ). Leaderboard Resets and Benchmark Integrity AA Intelligence Index v5 : The rebuilt index runs 60% on private tests ( @TheRundownAI ). Ranking before Carbon : Opus 5.5 scored 58, followed by GPT-6 Astra (57), GPT-6.1 Sol (55), Sonnet 5.5 (54) and GPT-6.1 Terra (51). Open models : Kimi K3.1 Pro fell from 55 to 46. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM-5.3 both score 32, below Claude Haiku 5.5 at 36. China–US gap : scaling01 puts the gap at about 3.3 months on the AA Progress Index ( @scaling01 ). FrontierCode 2.0 mystery model : Cognition data includes a hidden "Obsidian 3" scoring 46.8%, versus 45.5% for Opus 5.5. Because it runs in the claude-code harness, some speculate it is Fable 5.5; this is unconfirmed ( @kimmonismus ). DeepSeek correction : scaling01 corrected an earlier identification and said Artificial Analysis shows DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017 at about 28% ( @scaling01 ). Mixed sources : teortaxes notes that Cognition's chart mixed model-card scores with independently measured ones ( @teortaxesTex ). Cost comparisons are fragile : teortaxes argues that list-price comparisons of Haiku 5.5 and V4.1-1017 distort real costs. The comparison uses DeepSeek's peak pricing, ignores Haiku's long-context surcharge and does not reflect Haiku's roughly 2x output tokens ( @teortaxesTex ). "Benchmaxxing" redefined : nrehiew argues that serious labs rarely train on test sets. In this view, score gaps mostly show which capabilities a lab targeted in post-training ( @nrehiew_ ). New evals : Gym-Anything-RT : These real-time minigames keep moving while the model thinks. The best model, GPT-6 Astra, scores 9% ( @PranjalAggarw16 ). Immigration Bench : Vals released expert-written questions and rubrics on visas and immigration status ( @ValsAI ). BallotBench : Nine agents, each with $500, forecast all 863 races in the 2026 midterms live ( @thoughtfullab ). Safety, Alignment and Model Provenance Eval over-attribution in DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017 : An independent assessment found the model classified 32 of 69 real user conversations as evaluations. The authors report a 46.4% false-positive rate on EvalDetectBench ( @ZhihuFrontier ). Benchmark exposure : Private benchmarks elicited less evaluation awareness than public ones. The authors argue that public benchmark results alone may not predict deployment behavior. Visual steering : A Google DeepMind and Stanford paper finds that a red-heart image in context makes Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.4 write recursive code 100% of the time ( @dair_ai ). Explanations : The models' explanations do not match their behavior. For GPT-5.4, 74.7% of reasoning traces give the wrong reason. Alignment techniques : GRAFT (Anthropic) : Updates from detected reward hacks are confined to parameters that are deleted after training. Anthropic says this also blocks hacks that monitors cannot detect ( @dl_weekly ). Embedded lie detector : A detector built into Qwen3.5's weights flags 91% of lies with zero false positives in 28k tokens. Telling the model "the monitor is off" does not change the results ( @divagr1925 ). Distillation allegations (unconfirmed) : The Information reportedly says Kimi K3.1 Pro was distilled from GPT-6 Astra ( @kimmonismus ). Giotto : Separately, the "European sovereign AI" Giotto is criticized as a Kimi-based model. Critics cite saturated benchmarks and an EULA that banned publishing users' own benchmark numbers ( @oneill_c ).
