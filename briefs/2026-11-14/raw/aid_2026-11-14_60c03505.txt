South Park Commons exists to do three things: Find outlier technologists Create the most talent-dense environment for them to expand their ambitions Invest with conviction, today, into the most important ideas of tomorrow We are maniacally focused on attracting and evaluating talent far ahead of success. We serve the most ambitious founders at (and ahead of) the formation stage. To do so, we first need to be able to recognize greatness when we see it. Most venture firms talk about the founders they want to work with as &#8220;spiky.&#8221; We agree but have always been unsatisfied by the lack of falsifiability, specificity, and repeatability of that term. We find that spikiness is more a &#8220;magnitude&#8221; than a clear &#8220;direction&#8221; to seek out. We want SPC members to spike , but not in a general sense; we seek out founders who clearly demonstrate excellence in five core dimensions: Curiosity Clarity Depth Magnetism Engine For each of these qualities, we&#8217;ll unpack both how we define the term and also illustrate how our north star of finding outlier talent has consistently led us to important themes and categories well ahead of the market. Our model of in-person community affords us the unique advantage of gaining area under the curve over many months to better understand how these qualities develop over time in our founders. We believe in slopes rather than y-intercepts. The most salient data points along that curve emerge from positive-sum and collaborative in-person relationships, not 2-3 meetings in a rushed process. Curiosity This is, in many ways, the foundation of SPC&#8217;s culture. People join us to explore their ambitions in a community of other curious people. Often, the missions people end up embarking on come from divergent conversations with people they would&#8217;ve otherwise never met. We seek variance in both our individual members and the emergent networks that form at SPC. We define &#8220;curious&#8221; people as: Generative: They are constantly tinkering. We care less about legible, prestigious experience and more about obsession. Original : They have a history of building unusual, non-incremental things (often, these are not businesses &#8211; they may be research papers, side projects, creative artifacts). Polymathic : They can connect insights across disparate domains. Clarity Being at &#8220;-1&#8221; does not mean that a founder lacks the ability to think clearly. Often they&#8217;re actually just breaking fundamentally new ground. They&#8217;re surfing technological waves that haven&#8217;t yet existed. Because we spend our time exclusively at the frontiers of technology, we have found it even more critical to search for founders who reduce complexity in pursuit of the most important (and often less sexy) solutions. Founders with clarity: Operate with epistemic humility : They can update their weights rapidly with new information, which is increasingly important in today&#8217;s compressing timelines for technological progress. Know what matters : They can answer &#8220;what matters most right now?&#8221; across technology, product, and business. Historical perspective : They are students of the game. In their free time, from a place of authentic curiosity, they study the greats and learn from their judgement. Depth A small handful of founders will, through obsession, vision, and execution, bend the world to their will. Far before they actually do that, these founders almost always show patterns of being able to break new ground and go deep in domains that others might find non-obvious. We decompose &#8220;depth&#8221; into three pillars. Technical : They operate with out-of-distribution capabilities and have an ability to solve hard technology problems quickly and well. Their intuition stems from authentic insight and earned experience. Product : They have great taste and craft. They can spot (and produce) exceptional work. They make non-obvious decisions with real tradeoffs that they are excited to articulate. Commercial : They know how money flows in their space and where value can be captured (as well as who is capturing it today). They are obsessive about their customers and are open-minded about inventing new business models to meet an evolving ecosystem. Magnetism As we bring people into SPC and work with them, in-person, every day, we build incredible longitudinal signal to inform our investment decisions. In particular, we pay extreme attention to who other members in the community gravitate toward. Even before they have a clear idea, outlier founders naturally pull peers into their orbit and demonstrate natural leadership. We look for: Storytelling : They are able to distill their vision into real artifacts, and, even if they don&#8217;t know what they want to work on, they can compellingly articulate their own life story. They are perceptive and can adapt to their audience. Authentic charisma : Great talent will find excuses to be around them. They don&#8217;t necessarily attract everyone, but they do command respect and admiration. Idiosyncrasy : Ideally, they are uniquely magnetic &#8212; i.e., they are not just yet another fundable or smart individual. There&#8217;s something about them that is off the beaten path; like individuals with &#8220;curiosity,&#8221; they are original. Engine Time and time again, we have found that the best companies are often overlooked for years at a time until the world wakes up, seemingly overnight. To persevere during these extended periods of rejection, great founders need both the initial sparks of courage to get started and endurance to grind through the fog of war. To evaluate engine, we seek: High agency : They do not ask for permission and behave like the main characters in their own lives. Their decisions stem from a source of internal self-motivation. Will to win : They take risks. This can show up in unusual life decisions, precocious career trajectories, and failures that they own (and are often proud of). They are competitive and hungry. They have a chip on their shoulder and have something to prove. Stamina and grit : They don&#8217;t stop when they hit a speed bump. They have shown the ability to stick with problems and ideas for longer than others would deem reasonable. Founder-driven Thesis Building We talk about curiosity, clarity, depth, magnetism, and engine in almost every conversation we have internally at SPC. This framework pervades how we think about discovering and supporting our companies. Timing matters. You cannot build something future-facing simply by investing into a category after it is clearly &#8220;hot.&#8221; By focusing on exceptional founders and following the gradients of their curiosity, we&#8217;ve been able to be both right and early .
