How LLMs, reasoning models, and agents relate and fit together As a small side note, and since I often get asked how reasoning models relate to today&#8217;s AI agents, here&#8217;s a short section on this before getting started with the videos. A reasoning model is essentially an upgrade from a conventional LLM, that is, adding more post-training via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (and optionally inference scaling techniques) to make it more capable of solving more challenging problems. Classically, reasoning models are developed for math and code, but the capabilities can generalize to other problem domains as well. A reasoning model then functions as the engine inside an agent harness, where the agent harness is a software or tooling framework around the LLM/reasoning model to tackle even more challenging problems (bigger coding tasks, travel booking, and all other sorts of personal assistant tasks). The task of the harness is sophisticated context management and giving the LLM access to tools. Figure 1: An engine analogy for conventional LLMs, reasoning models, and agent harnesses. Figure from my Components of a Coding Agent article. In this analogy depicted in the figure above, the model is the engine, and the agent harness adds the machinery that lets us use it for various things (as an upgrade from a simple chatbot interface). An agent can use either a conventional LLM or a reasoning model, but reasoning models are the more typical choice as they are more capable. (As mentioned above, most current-gen LLMs are reasoning models with variable reasoning effort settings.) So, the bottom line is that reasoning models, as the &#8220;engine,&#8221; remain an important part of understanding modern LLM agents.
