A recent trend we&#8217;ve covered is of tech companies moving to open models to rein in AI spend: Uber cut its per-token costs by 50%, Pinterest by 90%, and even AT&amp;T has achieved a 56% decrease. But how do you move from a frontier lab&#8217;s APIs to using open models? There are two options: &#8220;Bare metal:&#8221; With plenty of time and money, you can go &#8220;bare metal&#8221; and purchase a few dozen NVIDIA GPUs and use those AI supercomputers, like Revolut has done : running 56 separate B200 units itself to serve its inference needs. This is a lot of work to set it up, but after that, operational costs are as cheap as it gets. Inference provider: most companies contract with an inference provider that operates large fleets of GPUs, and makes it easy for customers to onboard and start using open models fast. Onboarding to an inference provider to run LLM workloads comes with challenges, such as dealing with tail latencies, worrying about cache hit rates, rate limits, and more. Inference engineering is a set of engineering practices that we believe is increasingly beneficial for software engineers to familiarize themselves with. To go deeper into this area, we&#8217;ve talked with three leading inference providers to learn more: Modal , Fireworks AI , and Baseten . What are inference providers &amp; how do they differ from AI labs? Inference providers are companies that sell access to LLMs which they run on users&#8217; behalf. From the outside, this isn&#8217;t so different to what OpenAI and Anthropic offer: an API endpoint, ability to stream tokens, and pay based on token usage. But what makes inference providers different from frontier labs is the models running behind the endpoint: inference providers run open-weight models, whereas frontier labs train their own models. In this field, Modal, Fireworks, and Baseten each operate slightly different strategies: Modal sees itself as a cloud infrastructure provider that focuses on making its offering programmable via its SDKs. It natively supports inference services through its SDKs, but leans heavily on the open source ecosystem for running its inference workloads efficiently. Performance tweaks to its stack are merged upstream. In the words of Richard Gong, Inference Lead at Modal: &#8220;when you run code on Modal, you should be able to see all the code that&#8217;s running.&#8221; Fireworks positions itself as a provider for specialized AI workloads. Most of its business is in dedicated deployments, which often include fine-tuning and provision of latency guarantees. Much fine-tuning and post-training is available for self-service. Fireworks also offers forward-deployed researchers that can help with post-training custom models or components. Dmytro Dzhulgakov, Fireworks&#8217; CTO, says: &#8220;our tagline is specialized intelligence, which means we will tune everything from model to infrastructure for your particular use case.&#8221; Baseten describes itself as an all-in-one place to build agents. It starts with the basic infrastructure layer with uptime guarantees, flexible commercial terms, without vendor lock-in. Baseten builds additional layers for building agentic workloads on top of its inference platform. These additional layers include sandboxes, governance services, and self-serving, post-training functionality. To customers, inference providers offer LLM APIs that can be used as drop-in replacements for frontier labs&#8217; APIs. However, inference providers are not the same, as suggested above: Inference providers don&#8217;t pre-train their own foundational models . A single pre-training run for frontier-level LLMs can cost millions of dollars, and only hyperscalers and specialized frontier labs can afford it. Inference providers run post-training on open-weight models because it&#8217;s significantly cheaper. Competitive pricing. The main business of inference providers is offering open-weight models at competitive rates. Dedicated hardware. A big part of the appeal of inference providers is the ability to have dedicated hardware for users to interact with vendors on the infrastructure layer. Efficiency &amp; customization. Inference providers offer similar commoditized open models, and compete heavily on efficiency and customization services. As an example of efficiency, Fireworks announced its Ember-1 model in September 2026, that is post-trained on the Kimi K3 foundation, delivering a similar quality level but with 40% fewer reasoning tokens consumed. Consuming far fewer reasoning tokens gives Fireworks an edge on cost compared to a vanilla Kimi K3 model. In general, frontier labs have poor uptime, and this helps inference vendors win market share. Even though many inference vendors offer higher reliability guarantees than frontier models, inference providers can also go down, too.
