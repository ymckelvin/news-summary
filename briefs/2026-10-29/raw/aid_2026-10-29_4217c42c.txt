Fanning out multiple agents is common, as is the practise of engineers steering them in Slack or Teams. A &#8220;context layer&#8221; with a knowledge graph and memory is used for better investigation outcomes. Several tech companies built their own investigation agents instead of relying on vendors. Making agents accurate. Helpful approaches include verifying incident hypotheses with adversarial agents and a mix of models, as well as running evals and backtesting. However, measuring &#8220;accuracy&#8221; of AI SREs is tricky and highly specific to each company, and even engineering team. Making agents fast &amp; affordable. Everyone wants fast, cheap, and accurate AI SREs, but there are tradeoffs. Model routing, lean prompts, and tracking cache hit rate are ways to engineer a system with the right tradeoffs for your constraints. How long until autonomous agents? The fully autonomous agents demoed on conference stages are still not a reality, but AI agents triaging pager calls &#8211; and silencing unimportant ones &#8211; is becoming more common. A new category of AI-related outages is appearing, and engineers should eventually get hit with fewer pages, but the incidents that do reach engineers will be hard. Why is AI SRE &#8220;hot&#8221;? A year back, there was little to no buzz about AI SREs, but that&#8217;s all been changing. The main driver seems to be that there&#8217;s a lot more code deploys. Almost all tech companies have seen their PR frequency double in a year due to AI agents. Let&#8217;s do some basic &#8220;reliability math&#8221;: i = c x p: I ncident rate = [ C hange rate] x [ P robability] C = number of changes per unit time P = probability a single change introduces failure This says that if we have few deployments (low change rate) and high confidence that each deploy works (low probability of failure), then that is a pretty stable system. But with AI agents, deploy frequency is up and the incident rate is likewise increasing at most companies. We know that C (the number of changes in a given timeframe) is up twofold or more on last year at many companies, and will probably climb higher. What does &#8220;AI SRE&#8221; mean? &#8220;AI SRE&#8221; looks on its way to becoming the agreed term for AI-augmented oncall tooling, even though we&#8217;ve not spoken to a single person who likes it &#8211; and we agree. AI SRE vendors themselves don&#8217;t like it. When we talked with incident.io, Resolve, and Rootly for this article, the reply was that, unfortunately, they&#8217;ve not come up with anything better and so this name has come to stick instead. Alternative names proved less sticky even though they&#8217;re less inaccurate, like &#8220;autonomous reliability agents&#8221;, &#8220;automated remediation agents&#8221;, and &#8220;automated incident investigation agents.&#8221; A reason &#8220;AI SRE&#8221; is less than ideal is as simple as the fact that a site reliability engineer (SRE) does a lot more than just deal with oncall alerts! They work on making a system more reliable in its entirety and a good chunk of that work happens before and after outages. Despite all that, this article is going with the flow and using &#8220;AI SRE&#8221; because there&#8217;s not much choice, while covering oncall and incident handling tools that are AI-augmented/AI-powered. We cover more on the SRE profession and reliability engineering in a deepdive with Dave Connor, who worked at Google when the term &#8216;SRE&#8217; was coined. AI SRE tools mostly help with the investigation phase of incidents. Tools like Rootly, incident.io, and ResolveAI are most helpful after an alert has fired and an engineer starts investigating. Pre-AI, this investigation phase involved: Alert details: inspect the alert and try to figure out what it means Dashboards : check custom dashboards to confirm whether there&#8217;s an alert, which systems are impacted, and where the root cause investigation should start, if so Runbooks : consult runbooks and follow the steps to remedy the issue, if it&#8217;s a common one Logs/observability : look through the logs of observability systems like Datadog, Sentry, Honeycomb, etc These days, AI tools have gotten pretty good at all these tasks if they have sufficient data access. Of the three vendors we talked with, all do their investigations with AI.
