AI Twitter Recap Top Story: DeepSeek DeepInfer&#8212;Production Inference, Open-Source Boundaries, and the Economics of Cached Tokens What happened DeepSeek released DeepInfer, the inference engine behind V4.1 Flash, alongside production-serving details that make the engineering report as important as the code. The release covers the inference engine and API-processing layer. DeepInfer supports distributed inference, speculative decoding, SWA Bounded Replay, and structured output on NVIDIA and Huawei Ascend platforms. The accompanying deepseek-recipe component implements production token encoding/decoding and official-API-compatible processing. DeepSeek says future model inference implementations and optimizations will also appear in this repository. @deepseek_ai , @deepseek_ai , announcement translation The reported production figures are substantial&#8212;but describe a heavily cached service. DeepSeek&#8217;s engineering report, as summarized and quoted in the supplied posts, reports 155 trillion tokens/day from one cluster , 96% input-cache hits , 400 ms average time to first token , and 230 tokens/s generation , reaching 300 tokens/s for coding . It also reports $38 revenue per accelerator-hour at off-peak prices . These are first-party production claims, not independently audited measurements. @ZhihuFrontier , @swyx &#8220;Fully open source&#8221; does not mean the entire production service is reproducible from this release. Although DeepSeek describes DeepInfer as fully open source, the technical account says the KV-cache management and load-balancing components are not yet fully released , because they remain coupled to internal infrastructure. The engine is open; important service-level orchestration remains a deployment responsibility. @deepseek_ai , @ZhihuFrontier vLLM supplied the most concrete comparative response. After acknowledging that DeepInfer was initially built primarily on vLLM and released under Apache 2.0 , the vLLM team reported decode throughput within 6% of DeepInfer&#8217;s published results on GB200 under a matched configuration. It also published AgentX results and reproduction recipes, materially qualifying claims that DeepSeek&#8217;s efficiency is inaccessible to existing open-source engines. @vllm_project , @vllm_project , reproduction resources The release triggered two distinct debates: whether DeepSeek has a uniquely strong serving stack, and whether its disclosures demonstrate high inference profitability more generally. The first now has comparative benchmark evidence; the second remains sensitive to workload mix, cache accounting, hardware cost, and which expenses are included. @Tim_Dettmers , @zephyr_z9 , @Yuchenj_UW Architecture: separate stages, schedule compute rather than tokens DeepInfer&#8217;s disclosed architecture separates vision encoding, prefill, and decode , allowing each stage to scale against its own bottlenecks. The recommended deployment shapes are ordinary eight-accelerator machines for prefill and 64&#8211;128-accelerator supernodes for decode . Decode uses wide expert parallelism to distribute MoE experts while maintaining large effective batches. These are deployment recommendations, not sufficient evidence of the exact hardware composition of DeepSeek&#8217;s production cluster. @ZhihuFrontier The scheduling details explain why a conventional &#8220;tokens per batch&#8221; comparison can miss substantial differences: Prefill scheduling estimates attention-compute cost. Work is allocated using estimated compute rather than raw request length alone. Chunk sizes vary to fit a time budget, instead of enforcing one fixed chunk size across heterogeneous requests. @ZhihuFrontier Prefill routing does not require cache affinity. Because cached prefixes are pooled in shared storage, a request can go to an available worker rather than the worker that previously served its prefix. In a detailed Chinese-language analysis, inference engineer Su Jin Yan highlights this as a particularly useful production design: prefill data-parallel groups balance on &#8220;compute tokens,&#8221; while shared storage supplies reusable state. The same analysis highlights request bucketing and FIFO scheduling with a runtime estimator. @SuJinYan123 Decode routing balances both running-request count and KV-cache length. Request count approximates scheduling pressure; accumulated KV length captures memory/state pressure that request count alone misses. The engineering commentary describes prefill/decode scaling signals as aggregate compute load for prefill and running requests for decode. @ZhihuFrontier , @SuJinYan123 Expert-parallel load balancing replicates popular experts. Replicas are periodically redistributed to reduce inter-device waiting caused by routing skew. The trade-off is additional HBM consumption against higher throughput, with diminishing returns as replication increases; the useful replication factor is workload-specific. @ZhihuFrontier , @SuJinYan123 CPU scheduling overlaps accelerator execution. Asynchronous scheduling removes idle gaps between decode steps rather than treating host-side orchestration as free. @ZhihuFrontier One technically notable observation is the apparent absence of tensor parallelism in the disclosed Blackwell configuration. A commentator contrasts this with default vLLM/SGLang recipes and traces the approach back to DeepSeek V3&#8217;s H800-era interconnect constraints.
