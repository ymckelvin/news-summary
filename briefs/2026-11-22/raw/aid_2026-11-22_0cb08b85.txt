Within a few hours of each other ( timeline ), two mathematicians claimed independent proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis, the most famous open problem in mathematics ( Scientific American ). Levent Alpöge of Anthropic first made his Claude-driven proof in a note released with Lean code (1.3GB worth, ~250,000 page equivalent) rather than a full paper. Elliot Glazer confirms that the formalized statement is GRH and puts the chance of a Lean bug well under 1%. So if it finishes compiling with no extra axioms, it is probably correct, and no human will ever have read all of it… …prompting Alessandro Masullo to release his 940 page, 100% human , work in progress proof he had been working on for the last 12 years (and had been undergoing secret manual review and revision per Kevin Hartnett ). This is the third in the trilogy of frontier math breakthroughs, following from the Navier Stokes solutions and the Quasi-Riemann bounding, but it doesn’t feel over. OpenAI's September Navier-Stokes claim is still the cautionary tale: critics argued that its Lean proof did not match the natural-language result. Both AI-claimed Millennium Prize results will take years to verify. Apart from the incredible accomplishment of the most famous problem in mathematics, one cannot miss the contrast that the AI-written solution is probably correct but incomprehensible , and the other solution is probably technically incorrect but understandable . AI Twitter Recap Claimed Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis with Claude The claim : Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says that, "working with Claude," he has found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis. He says the method also covers the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) ( Scientific American , statement summary , remark.pdf link ). Artifact : Only Lean code was released. A written-out prose version reportedly ran past 250,000 pages ( timeline ). Timeline : The work began on Oct 6. Eight of the 20 steps landed on Nov 15, and the final entry on Nov 16 reads "GRH." Alpöge credits Claude for the parts involving automorphic forms on higher-rank groups, an area where he says he has little mastery ( breakdown ). Unknowns : The Claude model used and the degree of human steering have not been disclosed ( open questions ). Verification status : Elliot Glazer confirms that the formalized statement is GRH. The Lean code is "still compiling, will take a few hours." He puts the chance of a Lean bug at &lt;&lt;1% ( analysis ). Provisional status : Artificial Analysis notes that both AI-claimed Millennium results are unverified: this one and OpenAI's September Navier-Stokes claim, which used an unreleased model ( tally ). Competing human proof : Alessandro Masullo has announced a separate, purely human proof of RH. It is reported as a roughly 940-page GRH draft ( announcement , page count ). Interpretation debate : Some argue the result shows we have little idea how hard math problems objectively are, rather than showing broad superhuman ability ( Theo Jaffee , Ege Erdil ). Brute-force framing : Others compare the approach to olympiad-style "bashing" at a scale humans lack the willpower for ( cloneofsimo ). Jaggedness : Omar Khattab notes the contrast between solving Millennium problems and being "this dumb on 1000 other things" ( post ). Market reaction : ANT stock fell about 10% on the day ( scaling01 , Nabeel Qureshi ). Unconfirmed rumor : A DeepMind "math horizon" repo appearing the same day prompted speculation about Gemini math results ( unconfirmed ). Thinking Machines Releases Inkling 2 Mini, an Open-Weights Looped Transformer Inkling 2 Mini : A 26.4B-parameter looped MoE under Apache 2.0, pitched as small enough for a laptop. It scores 20.1 on the AA Index ( launch , Mira Murati ). Architecture : There are 12 shared core layers run for 3 passes, with gated messages carried between passes ( vLLM ). Active parameters : 3.4B unique active parameters, or 5.4B effective counting the loops. It has a 1M context window and accepts text and image input ( AA ). Training recipe : Two-thirds of the experts were pruned during mid-training, followed by logit distillation from a larger Inkling ( eliebakouch ). Claimed results : It leads open models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%) ( via teortaxes ). Comparison to Inkling 1.0 : It reportedly beats Inkling 1.0 at 2.7% of the size ( Ziqiao Ma ). Independent evals : Vals : It ties Claude Opus 5.5 on CyberBench at under a quarter of the cost and keeps 96–97% of Inkling 2 Small's Vibe Code and SAGE scores ( Vals , details ). Artificial Analysis : AA places it on the Pareto frontier for intelligence vs. active parameters ( AA ). ARC-AGI-2 : It reportedly scores 49.3% at $0.14/task, well below Inkling 2 Small ( teortaxes ). Safety and interpretability rationale : Thinking Machines says the open release is meant to let researchers study how loops affect chain-of-thought monitorability ( Alex Robey ). Variable-loop model : A companion research model, TML-d24-varloop-research, shows long-context scores (GraphWalks, MRCR v2) improving with more passes. Knowledge benchmarks such as SimpleQA and AA-Omniscience do not improve ( Ziqiao Ma ). Goodfire analysis : Goodfire mapped information flow across layers, blocks and loops ( Goodfire ). NDIF hosting : NDIF hosts both models with tooling for activation patching across loops ( NDIF ). Serving and local availability : vLLM had day-0 support. GB200 decode : TP1 decode on GB200 drops from 10.29 to 3.84 ms at batch 1, with kernels per step falling from 4,278 to 927 ( numbers ).
