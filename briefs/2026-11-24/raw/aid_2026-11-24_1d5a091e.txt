OpenAI put its pretraining loss curve on the record. In Perplexity and Intelligence , Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki plotted base-model log-perplexity back to GPT-3.5 (on their internal held-out coding dataset … which has notable caveats ): View post on X ↗ Think of it as how surprised the model is by the next token on a held-out dataset. Lower is better. GPT-3.5 was around 0.99 and GPT-4 around 0.91. When OpenAI put a model around 0.76 (GPT6 Astra) to work on open math problems, hundreds of frontier math problems started falling quickly . Pachocki extrapolates that another jump in capabilities could come around 0.68–0.70. This is all in context of pacing the frontier . Pachocki proposes that labs agree on a first limit to development and spell out what would let them cross it, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitoring. Microsoft’s Mustafa Suleyman gave the first win by cosigning and joining in, running MAI-2 Base on OpenAI’s evaluation set and reporting 0.819. We’d need at least a couple more top labs to join this effort for it to gather steam as a real self-regulatory limit, most notably of course, Anthropic. Enquiries and feedback to business@latent.space AI Twitter Recap OpenAI Publishes Its Pretraining Perplexity History and Proposes Shared Development Limits The disclosure : Jakub Pachocki's essay "Perplexity and Intelligence" publishes five years of OpenAI base-model log-perplexity on a fixed held-out dataset. It argues for industry-wide limits based on perplexity and other measurements, with further progress gated on milestones in understanding and monitoring AI behavior, such as a successor to chain-of-thought (CoT) monitors ( essay thread ). Policy framing : Sam Altman quoted the proposal for a first common limit with concrete exit criteria ( quote ). He tied it to the standards commitments in the White House Accords ( accords ). Endorsements : Greg Brockman highlighted the warning that alignment progress is unlikely to keep pace with pretraining acceleration ( gdb ). Mark Chen called the numbers formerly "sensitive IP" and asked other labs to disclose their key metrics ( markchen90 ). Claimed findings : Losses below 0.8 coincided with emergent capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics ( excerpt ). Thresholds and timing : OpenAI expects further "transformative capabilities" in the ~0.68–0.7 window and describes a steep acceleration in 2026 ( summary ). Internal model : An unreleased internal model already sits below GPT-6.2 Astra. Pachocki says pretraining could see an "avalanche" like the recent one in math as soon as 2027 ( scaling01 ). Community extrapolations (unofficial) : Dan Hendrycks infers ~3–5×/yr compute-efficiency gains since GPT-4, possibly 100×+ this year ( hendrycks ). One fit, using the internal model at 0.732 against 0.761 for the released model, reaches 0.70 between January and March 2027 ( fits ). Microsoft joins : With OpenAI's permission, Microsoft AI reported MAI-2 Base at 0.819 on OpenAI's eval set. It shares OpenAI's tokenizer and lands in GPT-5.6 Sol base-model territory. Mustafa Suleyman suggested bits-per-byte for future cross-lab comparisons ( mustafasuleyman ). Open-model mapping : Elie Bakouch used MAI as a bridge to place open models on OpenAI's curve ( plot ). He labels the further chaining through Inkling bpb a "very weak approximation" ( details ). Lucas Beyer's caveat : Inkling's held-out codebase may be largely agent-generated, which would make bridge-based placements look too optimistic ( giffmana ). Methodological critiques : Critics raised several confounds: Tokenizer bias : Common limits could favor OpenAI over Anthropic because of tokenizer differences ( davidmanheim ). Post-training compute : Post-training likely accounts for more than 75% of FLOPs, so pretraining perplexity may be the wrong pacing metric ( stochasticchasm ). Synthetic data : Part of the steep curve may come from a growing share of model-generated pretraining data ( Azaliamirh ). Missing context : Without model sizes or compute figures, it is hard to separate scaling from algorithmic progress ( Grad62304977 ). OpenAI's response : Staff say the eval is fixed over time and excludes model-generated code ( johnohallman ). Will Depue added that it is "held out, frozen, tokenizer agnostic" ( willdepue ). Pressure on other labs : Several researchers asked OpenAI to release the held-out set ( oneill_c ; DimitrisPapail ). Anthropic's redactions : Anthropic redacted pretraining metrics from its METR report ( eliebakouch ), prompting calls to publish them ( _NathanCalvin ). Third-party verification : METR offered to verify pause or compute-allocation claims under its standard terms ( ChrisPainterYup ).
